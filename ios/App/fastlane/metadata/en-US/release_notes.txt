Working with expressions in the reader got a lot better this time.

- You can now grow an expression. Tap the word next to one you have already translated and the two join into a single expression, instead of having to ungroup and rebuild it by hand.
- Every join you make gets its own unlink mark, right where the join is. Tapping it separates just that one, so you can take an expression apart a piece at a time instead of undoing the last join wherever it happened to be.
- Fixed: unlinking an expression could drop a word from the page entirely. If you fused two expressions and then took them apart, part of the sentence went missing.
- Fixed: after widening an expression, the translation you got back was for the old, narrower one.
- While a separated piece waits for its translation, it now holds its place with a blinking chip, so the line no longer jumps and settles.
- Tap a translated word to hide its translation. The little eye icon on the chip is gone; the word itself is the switch now.
- Translated words are easier to read. The orange was very light against the cream background, so text and underline are a darker shade that meets the accessibility contrast standard.
- Underlines stop at the word they belong to. Two expressions side by side used to share one unbroken line and read as a single unit.

In the feed:
- The topic now sits above the headline as a quiet label, the way a newspaper sets a section over a story, with the source on a single line underneath. The headline is the loudest thing on the card again.
- Tapping that topic narrows the feed to it, rather than opening the article by accident. Back undoes the filter.
