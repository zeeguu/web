Pick your variety of a language, and know when a text was written by a machine.

Language varieties:
- If you are learning Dutch or Portuguese, you can now say which variety you want - Belgian or Netherlands Dutch, Brazilian or European Portuguese. Pick it while signing up, or later in Language Settings. "Any" stays the default, and is what everyone has today.
- The choice is a row of pills with a flag on each, right above your level, so you can see every option without opening anything.
- A variety is a filter, so it can leave you with an empty feed on a quiet day. When that happens the feed now says which country it is filtering for, instead of showing a blank page, and offers a way straight to the setting. If a topic is also selected, it names that too, so you are not sent to the wrong screen.
- Your level now belongs to the language you chose: switching languages re-seeds it instead of quietly saving your Danish level under Portuguese.

Knowing what a machine wrote:
- Article cards now say when their text is machine-written: "AI summary" for a summary we generated, "AI-simplified" for a text adapted to your level. Texts your teacher or a friend uploaded carry no such mark, because their words are their own.
- The reader now names the level a text was written to, rather than calling it "your level" - which stopped being true the moment you changed your setting.
- The link back to the original article carries its level and, for a translated piece, the flag of the language it is in, so you know where following it lands you.

Also:
- Language Settings and signup now ask the same three questions with the same controls, so the level picker looks and behaves the same on both, and reads properly in dark mode.
