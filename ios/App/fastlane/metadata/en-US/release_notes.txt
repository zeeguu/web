This release is mostly for teachers and their students.

For teachers:
- My Texts and My Classrooms now look like one product, and read properly in dark mode.
- Filter your texts by class, with a count on each class and a "Not shared" pile for the ones that never went anywhere.
- Each text shows its classes as pills. Tap one to see the rest of what went to that class, or remove it with a single tap - no trip through the sharing dialog.
- The share-with-classes dialog now ranks classes sensibly and gains a search box once you have more than a handful.
- A class page has Students and Texts tabs, with every action on the class in one header.
- A class warns you when it holds texts in a language its students are not learning, since nobody in the class can see those.
- Sorting is a normal dropdown now: it closes when you click away, works with a keyboard, and is readable in dark mode. Added Title A-Z, and Newest and Oldest first.
- A text's difficulty is shown as one level, instead of our pipeline talking to itself.
- Deleting a class takes you back to My Classrooms instead of leaving you on a page that no longer exists.
- The Students tab got its label back, and Add Students no longer appears on a class that has no students yet.

For students:
- Your Classroom now respects the card style you picked in Settings - headlines, preview or interactive.
- If you are in more than one class, each text says which class it came from, and you can filter the list by class.
- Teacher-written texts no longer claim to come from a source called "userarticle".

Everywhere else:
- Profile tabs are real pages, so Back returns you to the tab you left instead of dumping you on Badges.
- Coming back from a friend's profile keeps your search, instead of emptying the box.
- Friend search results show the languages someone is learning and when they were last active, so you can tell apart two people with the same name.
- A simplified article's byline now says what it was simplified from, as in "Simplified to A1 from B2".
- When a friend shares an article, the dialog offers the level we will actually adapt to, and closing it now means neither option rather than quietly picking one.
- Your daily lesson is dated by the day it was made, not the day you happen to open it.
